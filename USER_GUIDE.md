# How to Read the Report — A User's Guide

This guide walks through every section of the PDF the tool generates,
in order, explaining what each element means and how a Jyotish reader
uses it. It assumes no prior knowledge, but flags the places where
tradition offers depth worth pursuing.

One framing note before anything else: the *positions* in the report are
exact astronomy; the *meanings* attached to them are the classical
Jyotish tradition's readings (Parashara for the natal chart, Neelakantha's
Tajika for the annual charts). The report is a study instrument within
that tradition, not medical, financial, or legal guidance.

---

## 1. The title block

**Ayanamsa** is the offset between the tropical zodiac (Western) and the
sidereal zodiac (Vedic) — about 22–24 degrees today, growing ~50 arcsec a
year. Raman and Lahiri are the two most common values, ~1.45 deg apart.
Sign and house placements almost never differ between them, but exact
degrees, some nakshatra-padas, and dasha timing can. The report states
which one it used; compare like with like.

**Whole-sign houses** means each house is one entire sign: the sign
rising at birth (the Lagna) is house 1, the next sign house 2, and so on.
This is the standard way to read the main (D-1) chart.

## 2. Graha Positions table

One row per graha (planet) plus the Lagna (ascendant — the sign rising
on the eastern horizon at birth; the anchor of the whole chart).

* **Rashi / Deg** — the sidereal sign and degree within it (0–30).
* **Nakshatra–Pada** — the 27 lunar mansions divide the zodiac into
  13°20' arcs; each splits into four *padas*. The **Moon's** nakshatra
  matters most: it names your *janma nakshatra* and seeds the entire
  Vimshottari dasha clock (section 6).
* **H** — house occupied, counted from the Lagna.
* **Dignity** — the planet's comfort in its sign:
  *Exalted* (peak strength) > *Own sign* > *Friend's sign* > *Neutral* >
  *Enemy's sign* > *Debilitated* (weakest). **Dig bala** appended means
  the planet also stands in its strongest *house* (Sun/Mars in the 10th,
  Jupiter/Mercury in the 1st, Moon/Venus in the 4th, Saturn in the 7th).
  Strong dignity means the planet delivers its significations cleanly;
  weak dignity means with friction. **(R)** marks retrograde motion.

**Sandhi caution** — a planet within 1 degree of a sign boundary. A small
birth-time error or a different ayanamsa could move it into the next
sign, changing its dignity, house, and divisional positions. Treat any
fine-grained claim resting on a sandhi planet as provisional.

## 3. The two diagrams

Both show the *same* chart in the two conventional formats:

* **North Indian** — *houses* are fixed on the page: house 1 is always
  the top-centre diamond, houses run counter-clockwise. The number
  inside each house is its sign (1 = Aries ... 12 = Pisces).
* **South Indian** — *signs* are fixed: Pisces is always top-left, and
  signs run clockwise; the Lagna cell is marked with a diagonal slash.
  Count houses clockwise from the marked cell.

Learn to read both; India's literature uses each freely.

## 4. Yogas & Combinations

Named planetary patterns the classical texts single out. The report
detects a documented rule set — highlights:

* **Gaja-Kesari** (Moon–Jupiter in mutual kendra): protected mind,
  reputation. **Budha-Aditya** (Sun+Mercury together): analytical
  authority. **Pancha Mahapurusha** (Ruchaka/Bhadra/Hamsa/Malavya/Sasa —
  one of the five non-luminaries own-sign or exalted in a kendra):
  a first-rank dignity signature.
* **Chandra yogas** (Sunapha/Anapha/Durudhara): planets flanking the
  Moon support it; **Kemadruma** (no flankers) is a lonely Moon — the
  report also checks the standard cancellations and says so.
* **Yogakaraka** — for certain Lagnas one planet owns both the best
  trine and the best kendra (Saturn for Taurus/Libra, Mars for
  Cancer/Leo, Venus for Capricorn/Aquarius) and becomes the chart's
  single most benefic agent. Weigh everything it touches accordingly.
* **Flags, not verdicts** — *Kuja dosha* (Mars in certain houses) and
  *combustion* (a planet too close to the Sun: outwardly weakened,
  inwardly intensified) are listed as cautions for further assessment,
  not conclusions.

## 5. Graha Drishti (aspects)

Vedic aspects are cast by **whole house**, not by degree orb. Every
planet aspects the 7th house from itself (opposition); three cast extra
aspects: **Mars** the 4th & 8th, **Jupiter** the 5th & 9th, **Saturn**
the 3rd & 10th. The table shows, for each house, who sits in it and who
aspects it — the numbers in parentheses are the aspect cast.

How to use it: a *mutual* aspect (both planets aspect each other) is a
standing dialogue between their significations. An **empty house with
many inbound aspects is still highly active** — activation by drishti is
as real as occupation. A powerful cluster with *no* benefic aspect runs
"raw"; a difficult conjunction aspected by Jupiter runs "supervised."

**What each aspect implies.** Under the table, every drishti the chart
casts is read out one at a time, grouped under the planet casting it:
what that planet's gaze does to whatever it falls on, applied to the
karakatva of the planet (or the Lagna) it looks at and to the house
those matters work out through. Each one carries a grade —
*supportive*, *mixed* or *testing* — computed from the aspecting
planet's natural nature (benefic/malefic; the Moon counts as a benefic
waxing and a malefic waning), its dignity and combustion, and its
natural friendship toward what it aspects. Read the grade as weight,
not verdict: a single testing aspect is not a problem, a convergence of
them on one planet is a theme. Three cautions the classics insist on and
this section repeats: a planet **nobody** aspects is unsupervised rather
than free; Mercury is graded a benefic here though the tradition has it
take the colour of its company; and conjunction is not drishti — for
planets sharing a house, read the occupants column.

## 6. Vimshottari Mahadasha

The 120-year planetary period cycle, seeded from the natal Moon's
nakshatra. The **Mahadasha (MD)** table shows the nine major periods of
the life; the **Antardasha (AD)** table breaks the report window into
sub-periods. A period activates its lord: the lord's *house, sign,
dignity, and aspects in the natal chart* describe the flavor of those
years. Reading an AD: blend the MD lord (the era) with the AD lord (the
chapter), each through its natal condition.

Timing caveat: dasha dates inherit every input sensitivity — birth-time
error and ayanamsa choice both shift them. If the Moon sits near a
nakshatra boundary (see sandhi flags), treat all dates as +/- months.

**Navigating each Mahadasha** follows the table: the same reading — the
lord's natal house, dignity, Vimshopaka score and the aspects on it —
applied to every era of the timeline, with the age span it covers and
whether it has already run, is running now, or is still ahead. Read the
past eras backwards, as a check on whether the chart describes the life
so far; read the coming ones as ground to prepare rather than to act on.
Rahu and Ketu own no sign, so their eras are read through the house they
occupy and through the lord of that sign. The 'Dasha Now & Next'
section (printed when an as-of date is given) then zooms in on the
running MD/AD/PD for that one date.

**The Week Ahead** (also as-of driven; the first, folded section of the
GUI's Dasha tab) drops one more level, to the Monday-to-Sunday week
containing that date. It is classical *gochara*: every transit counted
from your natal **Moon**, graded by the standard favourable-house table
(3/6/10/11 for the Sun, 3/6/11 for Mars, Saturn and the nodes,
2/5/7/9/11 for Jupiter, and so on), and weighted by the Ashtakavarga
bindus of the sign being transited — 30+ is strong ground, 24 or fewer
thin. The day table follows the transit Moon, which changes sign every
2–3 days: days in the 1st, 3rd, 6th, 7th, 10th or 11th from your natal
Moon carry outward moves best, and the 8th — *chandrashtama* — is the
day the tradition says to keep light. Vedha, the obstruction rule that
cancels some transit results, is deliberately not applied.

Read it under the dasha, never over it: the dasha says which promise is
switched on, the week only says what the weather is while it runs. A
favourable week inside a strained period is a good week for that
period's work, not a different life.

**What to do with it** turns that reading into scheduling: which days
carry a signing, an interview or an apology best; which day to keep
clear; which stretch of the week suits paperwork, which suits hard
physical work, and which asks you to slow down on the road or hold a
purchase. Each line comes from one graha's classical counsel, sharpened
by the bindus of the sign it is transiting. They are suggestions about
posture, not forecasts of events — where one disagrees with your own
judgement, the tradition's own advice is to wait for a second
indicator.

## 7. The Twelve Houses table

For each house: its sign, occupants, where its **lord** went, and its
classical significations. The lord's destination matters as much as the
occupants: an empty house is *read through its lord* — "2nd lord in the
9th" ties wealth to fortune/father/dharma even with nobody home in
the 2nd.

## 8. Tajika Varshaphala (annual charts)

One chart per requested year, cast for the instant the Sun returns to
its natal degree (the **pravesh** — the "solar birthday," rarely at the
birth clock time). Read it as the year's weather over the natal chart's
climate. Elements:

* **Varsha Lagna** — the sign rising at pravesh. The report also names
  which *natal* house is rising: that house's themes tone the year.
* **Muntha** — a progressed point: the natal Lagna advanced one sign per
  year of age. Its **house in the varsha chart** is the year's headline,
  graded per Neelakantha: houses **1, 9, 10, 11 excellent; 2, 3, 5 good;
  4, 6, 7, 8, 12 adverse**. The Muntha lord's condition modifies the
  grade — a well-placed lord substantially rescues an adverse Muntha.
* **Year-lord candidates** — the *panchadhikari* offices (Muntha lord,
  Varsha-Lagna lord, Janma-Lagna lord, Dina/Ratri-pati) with a dignity
  hint each. Full Panchavargiya Bala is **not** computed; pick the
  strongest candidate yourself — dignity, kendra placement, and double
  candidacy (one planet holding two offices) are the usual tiebreakers.
* **Mudda dasha** — the Vimshottari sequence compressed into the solar
  year: your month-by-month calendar. The year-lord's own mudda period
  and Jupiter/Venus periods are conventionally the bright windows;
  Rahu/Saturn periods the demanding ones — always modulated by those
  planets' condition in the varsha chart.

* **The year's outlook, themes and suggestions** — those same rules,
  applied: the natal house rising sets the frame, the Muntha's house and
  grade the headline, the Muntha lord's and year-lord's condition in the
  annual chart the tone, and each Mudda stretch is graded by *its* lord's
  condition there. That grading is what makes one stretch the place to
  put a deliberate move and the next one a place to hold steady, which
  is what the suggestions say. Sade Sati running at the pravesh is
  called out, and it outweighs the annual chart.

Unless you name the years yourself, the current year and the next one
are cast — the two anyone actually reads. Age 0 has no varsha chart:
the natal chart itself governs the first year.

## 9. Transit calendar & Sade Sati

Sign-ingress dates for the slow movers (Jupiter, Saturn, Rahu) across
the report window, with the **natal house** each ingress activates.
Classical gochara weighs transits from the natal **Moon** as much as
from the Lagna.

**Sade Sati** is Saturn's ~7.5-year passage over the 12th, 1st, and 2nd
signs from the natal Moon (first phase, core, final phase). The report
prints phase changes falling in the window. Read it as a period of
discipline and load, graded by Saturn's role in the chart — mildest when
Saturn is the chart's Yogakaraka or otherwise dignified.

## 10. Navamsa (D-9)

Each sign divides into nine parts of 3 deg 20 min; the D-9 chart maps
every planet to its navamsa sign. Tradition reads it two ways: as the
chart of **marriage and the spouse**, and - more generally - as how each
planet **matures**: a planet weak in the D-1 but dignified in the D-9
improves with age ("grows into itself"); the reverse warns of early
promise that needs guarding. **Vargottama** (same sign in D-1 and D-9)
marks a planet of special steadiness. The D-9 Lagna and its lord
describe the inner disposition behind the outer D-1 personality.

Degree sensitivity is at its maximum here: a navamsa is only 3 deg 20
min wide, so any planet the report flags as *sandhi* in the D-1 may
occupy a different D-9 sign under a small birth-time correction.

## 11. Ashtakavarga

A point system: each of the seven classical planets (and the Lagna)
contributes benefic points (**bindus**) to signs, per fixed Parashari
tables. The **BAV** rows show each planet's own map (0-8 per sign); the
**SAV** row sums them (total always 337; average 28 per sign).

Primary use is **transit weighting**: a transit through a sign with 30+
bindus stands on strong ground and delivers more of its promise; through
a sign with 24 or fewer, thin ground - hard transits (Saturn, Rahu) bite
harder and benefic transits give less. Check the SAV of the sign Saturn
occupies during a Sade Sati phase before judging its severity. A
planet's own BAV in the sign it transits (4+ bindus is good) refines
this further.

## 12. Where human judgment enters

The tool computes; it does not synthesize. The craft the report leaves
to you, in rough order of learning: (1) weighing a planet's dignity
against its house; (2) letting aspects modify conjunctions; (3) blending
MD lord with AD lord; (4) stacking independent techniques — a claim
supported by dasha *and* transit *and* varshaphala at once is how the
tradition confirms a signal; any single indicator alone is weak.

## Appendix A: Verifying any report's birth data (do this first)

The most common defect in commercial Jyotish reports is a wrong timezone
or daylight-saving setting. It shifts the Lagna (and therefore every
house, the yoga list, and the dasha balance) while leaving planet SIGNS
untouched - so the error is invisible unless you check. The tool's
"Input Verification" box exists for this: it prints the IANA zone, the
resolved UTC offset, whether DST was active on the birth date, and the
birth instant in UT. If another report for the same birth shows a
different Lagna, compare UTC offsets before suspecting anything else.
(Real case: a purchased report for a May birth in California used UTC-5
instead of PDT's UTC-7 - two hours - turning a Virgo Lagna into Leo and
changing the detected yogas.) A second benign difference to know:
Rahu/Ketu can be computed as the MEAN node (this tool) or the TRUE node
(much commercial software); they differ by up to ~1.7 deg, occasionally
a nakshatra-pada, rarely a sign.

## Appendix B: Reading sections found in commercial reports

Full-service software prints many sections this tool intentionally does
not generate. What they are, so you can read any report:

* **Avkahada Chakra** - the Moon-derived birth constants (this tool now
  generates the core set: gana, yoni, nadi, varna, vashya, naming
  syllable). Used for naming and in the eight-fold kuta match-making.
* **Bhav Chalit / Bhav table** - a second, unequal house system laid
  over the whole-sign chart (cusp-based). A planet near a cusp can
  occupy different houses in the two systems; traditions differ on
  which to prefer for which question.
* **Upagrahas (Gulika, Mandi, ...)** - calculated shadow points from
  weekday/Sun formulas, used mainly in horary and fine-grained work.
* **Shodashvarga** - the sixteen divisional charts, each labeled with a
  life area (D-2 wealth, D-3 siblings, D-7 children, D-10 career,
  D-12 parents, D-30 misfortune, D-60 totality...). This tool ships
  D-9; the others follow the same reading logic: dignity in the varga
  refines that life area.
* **Ashtakavarga Shodhana / Shodhya Pinda** - the two classical
  reductions (Trikona, Ekadhipatya) applied to the bindu tables, used
  for advanced transit timing. The raw BAV/SAV (which this tool prints)
  is the layer most reading actually uses.
* **Panchadha Maitri** - the five-fold friendship table: natural
  friendship (fixed) combined with temporal friendship (chart-specific
  house relations) into a composite. This tool's dignity column uses
  natural friendship.
* **Sudarshan Chakra** - the chart read simultaneously from Lagna, Moon
  and Sun as three reckoning points.
* **Avasthas** - planetary "states" (infant/youth/old; awake/dreaming/
  asleep; delighted/distressed...) grading how fully a planet delivers.
* **Karakas & Jaimini section** - a parallel system: chara karakas
  (planets ranked by degree take roles - Atmakaraka the self, etc.),
  Arudha/Upapada lagnas, sign-based aspects. A separate grammar;
  don't mix its rules with Parashari reading casually.
* **Yogini / Tribhagi / Shat-Trimsha dashas** - alternative timing
  systems used alongside Vimshottari for confirmation.
* **KP section** - Krishnamurti Paddhati: a different ayanamsa,
  Placidus-style cusps, and star/sub-lord significators; a distinct
  school, mainly horary.
* **Canned predictions** - paragraph libraries keyed to single factors
  (planet-in-house, lord-in-house, nakshatra). Read them as raw
  material, not conclusions: they routinely contradict each other
  because each paragraph sees one factor in isolation, they apply
  adult template text regardless of age, and some mix Western tropical
  aspects into a Vedic report. The craft - weighing dignity, aspects,
  dasha relevance, and convergence - is exactly what template text
  cannot do.
* **Gemstone / remedy sections** - the traditional remedial layer
  (stones, mantras, metals keyed to chart factors). Cultural practice,
  not computation; this tool omits it by design.
